# Etoys

- Year: 1996
- Designer(s): Alan Kay, Dan Ingalls, Scott Wallace, Ted Kaehler, John Maloney, Andreas Raab
- Paradigm(s): object-oriented, visual, event-driven
- Family: educational-visual

## Problem It Aimed to Solve

Etoys was developed by Alan Kay and others, drawing on their research at Xerox PARC and on Seymour Papert's constructionist theory of education, to create an environment in which even children could intuitively learn programming concepts. Existing languages such as Logo and Smalltalk were highly expressive, but they were too abstract for children to handle easily. Etoys was built on Squeak, a Smalltalk implementation, and designed as a visual object-oriented environment in which objects are manipulated through tile-based scripts. Through its adoption by the One Laptop per Child project, it spread to educational settings around the world and went on to influence the later Scratch.

## Features

- A visual object-oriented environment built on Squeak, a Smalltalk implementation, in which everything is treated as an object
- Describes the behavior of objects by combining tile-based scripts rather than writing text
- Inherits turtle graphics, enabling intuitive drawing, animation, and simulation building
- An event-driven mechanism lets objects respond to mouse actions and sensor input in programs the user builds
- Was bundled as standard software in the One Laptop per Child (OLPC) project and distributed to primary education settings around the world

## Current Status

Etoys is positioned specifically for educational use and is not used for practical software development. While it no longer enjoys the reach of the former OLPC project, development continues within the Squeak/Etoys community, and it remains in use as teaching material for programming education and constructionist learning.
