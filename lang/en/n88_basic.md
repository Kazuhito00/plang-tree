# N88-BASIC

- Year: 1981
- Designer(s): NEC
- Paradigm(s): procedural
- Family: origin

## Problem It Aimed to Solve

N88-BASIC was the standard programming language that NEC (Nippon Electric Company) supplied for its PC-8800 and PC-9800 series computers. Built on Microsoft's BASIC interpreter, it was developed to add the extensions needed for Japanese home and business personal computers, including Japanese-language handling, FM sound synthesis, and graphics capability. Loaded automatically at startup as a ROM-BASIC, it became the first programming language many Japanese personal computer users ever touched. As the platform moved from floppy disks to hard disks and then to MS-DOS, extended versions such as DISK-BASIC were also supplied. Alongside the popularity of the PC-9800 series, it became the de facto standard BASIC within Japan.

## Features

- Built on Microsoft's BASIC interpreter, extended with Japanese-language handling such as Japanese text display and kana character input
- Equipped with hardware-control instructions specific to the PC-8800/PC-9800 series, including FM sound synthesis and graphics commands
- Bundled as standard ROM-BASIC on the machines and started automatically on power-up
- Extended versions were supplied as the surrounding environment changed, including DISK-BASIC for floppy disk use and later N88-BASIC(86) for MS-DOS
- Followed the traditional line-numbered BASIC grammar while incorporating its own instruction set built around Japanese-language processing
- Became the de facto standard BASIC interpreter within Japan alongside the popularity of the PC-9800 series

## Current Status

With the decline of the PC-9800 series and the shift to MS-DOS/Windows, practical use of N88-BASIC has essentially ended, and it is positioned as a language whose historical role is over. Today it survives through use on emulators among retro-computing enthusiasts and as a subject of historical research.
