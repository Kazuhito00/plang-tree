# Pascal

- Year: 1970
- Designer(s): Niklaus Wirth
- Paradigm(s): procedural
- Family: algol-pascal

## Problem It Aimed to Solve

In the late 1960s, ALGOL 60 was highly regarded academically, but its ambiguous specification and implementation complexity made it somewhat unwieldy for teaching purposes.

While involved in the international committee discussions on standardizing ALGOL 60 (the so-called ALGOL X effort), Niklaus Wirth felt a strong need for a language with a simpler, stricter grammar that could directly teach students the concepts of structured programming.

Programming education at the time tended to lean toward either COBOL, aimed at business data processing, or the unwieldy Fortran, and there was demand for a "small, clear language" that could teach the essence of algorithms. Pascal was designed to meet this educational need.

## Features

- Strong static typing and structured data definitions through record and enumeration types
- Block structure delimited by begin/end, and structured control constructs such as if/while/for/case
- Syntax that clearly distinguishes procedures from functions
- A simple, readable grammar designed with education in mind
- A design that values type safety even while offering pointer types
- A simple program structure premised on a single compilation unit

## Current Status

Pascal has now lost its standing as a mainstream language for practical development and is a legacy language.

However, in the 1980s it went beyond its role as an educational language and was also widely used for PC application development via tools such as Turbo Pascal, and it is historically well regarded as a language that greatly contributed to the spread of structured programming. It still holds on today in some educational settings and in the maintenance of legacy systems.
