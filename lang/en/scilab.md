# Scilab

- Year: 1990
- Designer(s): INRIA (French National Institute for Research in Digital Science and Technology), École nationale des ponts et chaussées (ENPC)
- Paradigm(s): procedural, array, declarative
- Family: numeric-scientific

## Problem It Aimed to Solve

Around 1990, capable tools for scientific and technical computation and numerical simulation were limited to expensive commercial software such as MATLAB, leaving research institutions with few options they could freely use and modify. Researchers at the French institutions INRIA and ENPC developed an open-source platform built around a high-level, matrix-oriented language that could express complex numerical problems in relatively few lines of code. Originally named Ψlab (Psilab), it aimed at applications across a broad range of engineering fields, including signal processing, statistical analysis, and fluid dynamics. Development is now carried on by Dassault Systèmes, and it is used in both academia and industry.

## Features

- High-level syntax centered on matrix operations allows complex numerical problems to be expressed in relatively few lines of code
- Provides toolboxes (sets of extensions) for a broad range of engineering fields, including signal processing, control engineering, and statistical analysis
- Released as open source, allowing research institutions and companies to freely use and modify it
- Integrates a graphical block-diagram simulation environment (Xcos), enabling the modeling of dynamic systems
- Has a matrix-oriented language specification similar to MATLAB's, while developing its own distinct syntax and function ecosystem
