# Scratch

- Year: 2003
- Designer(s): MIT Media Lab (Mitchel Resnick et al.)
- Paradigm(s): educational, visual, event-driven
- Family: educational-visual

## Problem It Aimed to Solve

In traditional text-based programming education, syntax errors (typos, forgetting a semicolon, and so on) posed a major barrier for children, making it hard for them to focus on the programming concepts they were actually supposed to learn.

Mitchel Resnick and others at the MIT Media Lab, carrying forward the educational spirit of Logo's turtle graphics, aimed to create an environment where programs could be built simply by dragging and dropping blocks instead of typing code, and developed Scratch with this in mind. The goal was for children around the world to learn programming through creating and sharing their own creative works.

## Features

- Commands and control structures are visually represented as jigsaw-puzzle-like blocks
- The shapes of the blocks restrict which combinations are grammatically possible, making syntax errors essentially impossible
- Centers on event-driven descriptions, such as moving sprites (characters) and having them react to events
- Has a mechanism for sharing and remixing projects within an online community
- Can be applied to a wide range of creative activities such as animation, games, and interactive art
- Widely adopted as introductory programming material in schools around the world

## Current Status

Scratch has established itself as the worldwide standard for visual, block-based programming education, and it remains widely used today as teaching material for programming in primary and secondary education.

Its design philosophy of letting learners grasp programming concepts without being troubled by syntax errors has had a major influence on the way programming education itself is approached.
