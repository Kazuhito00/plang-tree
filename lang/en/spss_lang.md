# SPSS

- Year: 1968
- Designer(s): Norman H. Nie, C. Hadlai Hull, Dale H. Bent
- Paradigm(s): procedural, scripting
- Family: domain-specific

## Problem It Aimed to Solve

In the 1960s, researchers in the social sciences had to write their own programs, typically in FORTRAN, in order to perform statistical analysis—a major barrier for researchers without specialized programming knowledge. SPSS is a statistical analysis package developed by graduate students at Stanford University to let social scientists themselves carry out statistical analysis. Through its command syntax and a case-by-variable tabular data model, it made it possible to perform advanced statistical processing, including multivariate analysis, without specialized expertise. It was later acquired by IBM and continues to be widely used in the social sciences, market research, and healthcare fields.

## Features

- Centers on a tabular data model of cases (rows) by variables (columns), making the subject of statistical analysis intuitive to work with
- Its command syntax (SPSS Syntax) allows operations equivalent to GUI actions to be written and reused as scripts
- Provides advanced statistical methods—descriptive statistics, multivariate analysis, regression analysis, factor analysis, and more—as ready-made built-in procedures
- Automates the entire pipeline from data loading, transformation, and aggregation through to output, as a single batch process
- Supports both a GUI and command syntax, serving users ranging from non-programmers to statistics specialists

## Current Status

SPSS remains an actively used language, continuing to be widely applied as IBM SPSS Statistics in the social sciences, market research, healthcare, and education fields. Its accumulated library of statistical procedures and long-established user base keep it one of the standard choices for statistical analysis software.
