# Wolfram Language

- Year: 1988
- Designer(s): Stephen Wolfram
- Paradigm(s): functional, symbolic, rule-based
- Family: numeric-scientific

## Problem It Aimed to Solve

In the 1980s, symbolic computation (symbolic manipulation, differentiation, and integration of formulas), numerical computation, and graph plotting were often each handled by separate, dedicated pieces of software, forcing researchers to switch between different tools depending on their purpose. Stephen Wolfram, a theoretical physicist, wanted to create a language in which formulas, data, graphics, and even the program's own code could all be handled through a single, unified representation called a "symbolic expression." The Wolfram Language was developed under this philosophy of unified symbolic processing, and it was released to the world as the foundational language of the product Mathematica.

## Features

- Represents all data, formulas, and programs uniformly as symbolic expressions
- Has a computation model built around pattern matching and rule-based rewriting at its core
- Covers symbolic computation, numerical computation, visualization, and machine learning within a single language and environment
- Has thousands of built-in functions and access to external data
- Has been continuously developed for many years as the foundation of the commercial software Mathematica
